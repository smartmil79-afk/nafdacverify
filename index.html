FAQ: Spotting Counterfeits and Reporting

Practical checks before you buy or use a product. You can also ask the assistant (🤖, bottom right).

How to spot a counterfeit

1. Look for a NAFDAC registration number (NRN)

Registered products sold in Nigeria should carry a NAFDAC number. If there is none, do not buy it. A number alone is not proof: counterfeiters can copy real numbers, so go to step 2.

2. Verify the NRN and its status on the Greenbook

Search the number or product name at greenbook.nafdac.gov.ng. Check that the product name, manufacturer and strength match your pack, and look at the Status column. If the status is anything other than Active (for example Expired), the registration is not currently active, so ask the seller and check with NAFDAC. The Greenbook covers medicines, vaccines, devices, herbals, veterinary products and disinfectants; for foods, drinks and cosmetics use NAFDAC's registration portal.

3. Inspect the packaging closely
  • Spelling mistakes, blurry logos or colours that look off
  • Uneven or broken seals, loose caps, glue marks
  • Batch number and expiry date missing, smudged, or stamped on afterwards
  • Labels that peel or differ from a pack you know is genuine
4. Check the batch against official alerts

NAFDAC alerts often name specific brands, batch numbers and manufacturers. Search the product on the Banned Products page and read the full notice for what to look for.

5. Be careful where you buy

Prefer licensed pharmacies and established shops. Be cautious with street hawkers, unlabelled repackaged goods, and social-media sellers, especially for medicines. Prices far below normal are a warning sign.

6. Compare with a genuine product

If you can, compare weight, smell, colour, texture and taste with a pack you trust. If something is different, or you feel unwell after using it, stop and keep the pack.

Does "not listed" mean a product is genuine?

No. Alerts only cover products that have been reported and published. A product that is not on the list can still be fake or unregistered, so always do the Greenbook check.

Reporting a suspicious product

What should I do if I think a product is fake?
  • Stop using it, and seek medical help first if you feel unwell.
  • Keep the product, packaging and receipt. Do not throw them away.
  • Take clear photos: front, back, batch/expiry area and the NAFDAC number.
  • Note where, when and from whom you bought it.
  • Report it on our Report a Product page, and also contact NAFDAC directly (below).
How do I report to NAFDAC?

NAFDAC's website lists a complaints line: 0800-1-NAFDAC (0800-1-623322), and an enquiries line: 0700-1-NAFDAC (0700-1-623322). The Greenbook also links NAFDAC's Report SF (substandard and falsified products) form and an adverse drug reaction form. Contact page: nafdac.gov.ng.

Do I need an account to report here?

No. Anyone can send a report. Contact details are optional and only used if the team needs to follow up.

What happens to my report?

It is saved privately and reviewed by the ChecknVerify team. It is not published automatically, and a report alone does not make a product "counterfeit". Official confirmation comes from NAFDAC.

Is ChecknVerify part of NAFDAC?

No. ChecknVerify is an independent tool. Alerts and registration details come from NAFDAC's public pages, and each alert links to the official notice.

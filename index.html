Counterfeit alert19 Aug 2026 · Public Alert 041/2026

Fake toothpaste, one letter apart: ORACIRE+ vs ORACARE+

NAFDAC warned about a suspected counterfeit toothpaste sold under a name almost identical to a registered brand.

Read more ↓

NAFDAC says a toothpaste called ORACIRE+ is circulating in Nigeria under the guise of the registered ORACARE+. The alert followed a formal complaint and a market intelligence report. NAFDAC states that the origin, composition, safety and quality of the suspected counterfeit have not been verified, so nobody can say what is in the tube.

The alert is about the look-alike product only. It does not say the registered ORACARE+ is fake or unsafe.

What to take from it

  • Read brand names letter by letter. One changed vowel can be the whole difference.
  • Compare the pack with a tube you know is genuine, and check the NAFDAC number on the Verify Product tab.
  • If you find the look-alike, keep it and report it (see the FAQ for NAFDAC's reporting channels).

Source: NAFDAC Public Alert No. 041/2026

Falsified medicine19 Aug 2026 · Public Alert 042/2026

A registration number that doesn't exist: the Kano antimalarial seizure

A seized malaria medicine carried a NAFDAC number that NAFDAC says is fake.

Read more ↓

NAFDAC announced the seizure of suspected falsified Artemether/Lumefantrine 80mg/480mg, purportedly made by Bandaram Pharma Packleh Ltd (BPPL), India. The alert lists the registration number on the pack as 02-3457 (fake) and classifies the product as a Falsified (F) medical product. Press reports say it was intercepted during routine surveillance at a wholesale centre in Kano.

NAFDAC warns that falsified antimalarials can lead to treatment failure, a worsening illness and drug resistance.

What to take from it

  • A number printed on a box proves nothing by itself. Look it up, and make sure the product name, manufacturer and strength match the record.
  • Buy medicines only from licensed pharmacies.
  • Report suspected sales to the nearest NAFDAC office.

Source: NAFDAC Public Alert No. 042/2026

Substandard batch2025 · Updated Public Alert 030A/2025

Registered, but a bad batch: the ARTEMETRIN DS alert

A registered antimalarial can still have a batch that fails quality tests.

Read more ↓

NAFDAC's updated alert describes ARTEMETRIN DS (Artemether/Lumefantrine 80mg/480mg), a registered product labelled as made by A.C. Drugs Ltd in Enugu. NAFDAC reports that batch Q011G (manufactured 07/23, expiry 06/25) contained only 59.2% of the stated artemether and 71.2% of the stated lumefantrine, outside the expected range, and classed it as substandard.

Too little active ingredient can cause treatment failure and contribute to drug resistance.

What to take from it

  • An Active registration shows the product is registered. It does not guarantee every batch.
  • Check the batch number and expiry date on your pack against any alert for that product.

Source: NAFDAC Updated Public Alert No. 030A/2025

GuideEvergreen

How to read a NAFDAC alert in 60 seconds

Six things to look for before you decide whether an alert affects you.

Read more ↓
  • The exact product name. Look-alike names are common, and an alert may be about a copy rather than the real brand.
  • The manufacturer and NAFDAC number on the alert. Compare both with your pack. Some alerts point out that the number shown on the fake does not exist.
  • Batch numbers and dates. Many alerts cover specific batches only, not every pack of a brand.
  • The classification. Counterfeit and falsified mean the product is not what it claims to be. Substandard means it fails quality standards. A recall means a manufacturer or regulator is pulling product back.
  • What NAFDAC asks you to do. This is usually to stop using it, keep the pack, and report it.
  • The date. Check whether the notice has been updated.

A brand appearing in an alert does not mean all of its products are unsafe, and a product missing from the alerts is not proven genuine. Use the Verify Product tab to check the NRN and status too.

FAQ: Spotting Counterfeits and Reporting

Practical checks before you buy or use a product. You can also ask the assistant (🤖, bottom right).

How to spot a counterfeit

1. Look for a NAFDAC registration number (NRN)

Registered products sold in Nigeria should carry a NAFDAC number. If there is none, do not buy it. A number alone is not proof: counterfeiters can copy real numbers, so go to step 2.

2. Verify the NRN and its status on the Greenbook

Search the number or product name at greenbook.nafdac.gov.ng. Check that the product name, manufacturer and strength match your pack, and look at the Status column. If the status is anything other than Active (for example Expired), the registration is not currently active, so ask the seller and check with NAFDAC. If you cannot find a product, it may not be in the copy of the register yet, so search the Greenbook directly or ask NAFDAC.

3. Inspect the packaging closely
  • Spelling mistakes, blurry logos or colours that look off
  • Uneven or broken seals, loose caps, glue marks
  • Batch number and expiry date missing, smudged, or stamped on afterwards
  • Labels that peel or differ from a pack you know is genuine
4. Check the batch against official alerts

NAFDAC alerts often name specific brands, batch numbers and manufacturers. Search the product on the Banned Products page and read the full notice for what to look for.

5. Be careful where you buy

Prefer licensed pharmacies and established shops. Be cautious with street hawkers, unlabelled repackaged goods, and social-media sellers, especially for medicines. Prices far below normal are a warning sign.

6. Compare with a genuine product

If you can, compare weight, smell, colour, texture and taste with a pack you trust. If something is different, or you feel unwell after using it, stop and keep the pack.

Does "not listed" mean a product is genuine?

No. Alerts only cover products that have been reported and published. A product that is not on the list can still be fake or unregistered, so always do the Greenbook check.

Reporting a suspicious product

What should I do if I think a product is fake?
  • Stop using it, and seek medical help first if you feel unwell.
  • Keep the product, packaging and receipt. Do not throw them away.
  • Take clear photos: front, back, batch/expiry area and the NAFDAC number.
  • Note where, when and from whom you bought it.
  • Report it on our Report a Product page, and also contact NAFDAC directly (below).
How do I report to NAFDAC?

NAFDAC's website lists a complaints line: 0800-1-NAFDAC (0800-1-623322), and an enquiries line: 0700-1-NAFDAC (0700-1-623322). In its recent alerts NAFDAC also asks people to email sf.alert@nafdac.gov.ng, to use its Med Safety app.
